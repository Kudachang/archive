MONDELEZ — Past Project Experience
Status: Archived / no longer active
Period: 2025 (documented work)
Role: Executive Creative Director, agency-side (Publicis Groupe, China)
Brands: OREO and Chips Ahoy!
Purpose: Personal career and creative reference; not an active FUTURE™ project.
Scope of Work
- Led and developed creative thinking, campaign concepts, scripts, and pitch materials for Mondelez brands in China.
- Worked on OREO THINS, including the product proposition “Crispy yet Rich” (薄脆、浓醇) and social activation ideas for Xiaohongshu/Rednote. One concept used a playful contrast: a thin-looking dumbbell with surprising weight.
- Developed OREO China's 30th-anniversary and retainer-pitch thinking around playfulness and connection, with the platform “真的好玩在一块”. Explored parent–child interaction, cross-generational play, China-specific flavor references, and playful anniversary formats rather than nostalgia alone.
- Explored creative differentiation between OREO (participatory play) and Chips Ahoy! (play delivered to the audience).
- Worked through Mondelez's Voltage Test context, focused on consumer responses to TVC animatics. Two films were discussed, but their actual testing results and final outcomes were not verified in the available conversation record.
Working Context
- Heavy creative workload during a period of agency resource changes and limited team support.
- Required hands-on idea development and copy refinement alongside senior-level creative direction.
- Navigated multiple revisions and the gap between creative ambition, brand requirements, and executional resources.
Lessons Retained
1. Playfulness needs a concrete behavior. A campaign platform works better when people can actually participate, not merely hear a slogan.
2. Local relevance needs freshness. Chinese cultural references should feel current rather than relying on nostalgia or dated internet memes.
3. Test the actual execution. Consumer animatic testing should inform revisions, but unverified test outcomes should not be presented as proof of success or failure.
4. Protect scope and capacity. Creative leadership without sufficient resources can quickly become unsustainable hands-on production.
5. Keep the experience, close the project. The strategic and creative lessons remain useful; the account is no longer part of FUTURE™'s active pipeline.
Archive Note
This summary records work undertaken and lessons learned, not a claim that a pitch was won, a campaign launched, or particular Voltage Test scores were achieved. Those outcomes have not been independently confirmed here.
